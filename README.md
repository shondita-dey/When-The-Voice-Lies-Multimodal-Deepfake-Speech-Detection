# When-The-Voice-Lies-Multimodal-Deepfake-Speech-Detection


In recent years, significant progress in deep learning and speech synthesis technologies has enabled the generation of highly realistic artificial speech. Modern text-to-speech and voice cloning systems are capable of producing audio that closely resembles natural human voices in terms of pronunciation, intonation and emotional expression. While these advancements have improved the quality of human–computer interaction and accessibility tools, they have also given rise to serious concerns related to digital impersonation, misinformation and identity fraud through deepfake audio.
Deepfake speech refers to synthetically generated or manipulated audio that is designed to imitate the voice of a real speaker. Such audio can be used for malicious purposes, including financial scams, political manipulation, social engineering attacks and unauthorized identity verification. As these synthetic voices become increasingly indistinguishable from genuine speech, traditional methods of authentication and verification face growing challenges.
Existing deepfake speech detection systems mainly focus on analyzing low-level acoustic features such as pitch, spectral patterns and temporal artifacts. Although these approaches have demonstrated promising results, their effectiveness often declines when confronted with new synthesis techniques or unseen datasets. Moreover, audio-based detectors typically ignore the linguistic and semantic aspects of speech, which play a crucial role in natural human communication.
Human speech is not only characterized by sound quality but also by coherence, discourse structure and individual speaking styles. Real speakers exhibit consistent patterns in vocabulary usage, sentence construction, hesitation and topic development. In contrast, synthetic speech systems often lack long-term linguistic planning and contextual awareness which may result in subtle inconsistencies between the spoken audio and its textual representation.
In contrast, the linguistic content of speech transcripts offers an alternative and complementary perspective for deepfake detection. Transcribed speech generated from synthetic audio may exhibit subtle irregularities at the semantic, syntactic and stylistic levels, including semantic drift, unnatural word distributions, disrupted discourse flow and deviations in writing style. These linguistic inconsistencies are well-suited for analysis using NLP techniques and may persist even when acoustic cues are no longer reliable.
Despite this potential, NLP-driven deepfake detection remains relatively underexplored, particularly with respect to interpretability. This project addresses these challenges by proposing an NLP-focused deepfake speech detection framework that analyzes transcribed speech using semantic, syntactic and stylometric features. The system is designed to identify linguistic inconsistencies
indicative of synthetic speech while maintaining interpretability through the integration of Explainable AI (XAI) techniques such as feature attribution and instance-level explanations.
